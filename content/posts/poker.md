This year, the games have gotten a lot bigger.

Recently I've been doing a lot of thinking about what draws me to poker. Yes, the adrenaline is an obvious answer (there's a reason people get addicted to gambling... the feeling is very very real), and I only play with people I know so I get to spend time with them which is nice. But I think there is a more fundamental reason, and my friend Andy put it best: I like it when everyone has **skin in the game.** Life is only interesting when people have skin in the game. Good decisions must be rewarded and bad decisions must be punished, and no limit cash games just happen to provide a really good loss function for this (in theory).

The threshold for having skin in the game obviously varies from person to person. Everyone has a different relationship with money, and even modulo that everyone has a different tolerance for risk. I have a friend that's scared to call off a $5 bet with the second best hand, a friend that'll snap off a $500 bet with no hesitation if he believes it's optimal, and friends anywhere in between. This fear exists only because there is skin in the game. It is impossible to play poker without *something* on the line, no matter how much some people claim this, because stakes fundamentally change the texture of our decision-making.

Poker is poker because of the stakes. There is nothing particularly innovative about the way it's played and nothing particularly special about the rules—the strength of hands is literally ranked by the probability of them occuring. It's fun and interesting because *everyone* has something on the line. We call bluffs sometimes because being bluffed constantly hurts, not just emotionally but with respect to our loss function. As a corollary, having skin in the game is not something unique to poker—this is why I like playing games in general. Games are always more interesting when stakes are involved, and this is not because I have a gambling addiction.
